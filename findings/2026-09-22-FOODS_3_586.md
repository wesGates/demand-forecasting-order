## 01_explore — is the working set usable, and what is in it?

**1. Is the working set clean?**
Yes. No pre-launch rows were trimmed (the item is priced from the first day
of the history, 29 Jan 2011). Longest zero run is 10 days (WI_2, March 2013);
the availability screen warns at 30 and did not fire. Zero-sales share is
0.2% after the five Christmas closures per store were imputed (50 rows). The
remaining 43 zero days cluster on two dates shared by eight stores
(16–17 Feb 2012), a short run at TX_1 (14–19 Mar 2013) and WI_2 (16–25 Mar
2013): recording gaps or stocking gaps, not demand.

**2. What is the dominant structure?**
Weekly. At the busiest store the weekday means run Mon 88, Tue 84, Wed 85,
Thu 92, Fri 105, Sat 128, Sun 124 units: a 50% swing from midweek to the
weekend, and the shape is shared by every store once each is indexed to its
own mean. Autocorrelation at TX_2: lag 1 = 0.58, lag 7 = 0.61, 14 = 0.55,
21 = 0.53, 28 = 0.51, with lags 6 and 8 at 0.44–0.45 (a broad weekend peak).
That justifies lags 1, 2, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, rolling 7/28/56-day levels and
same-weekday means. An annual shape exists (August 1.21× the mean, January
0.81×, December 1.09×) on top of a multi-year drift: TX_2 averaged 109/day in
2011, 113 in 2012, 103 in 2013, 94 in 2014, 93 in 2015 and 82 in early 2016.
The overlay shows the years share a shape but not a level.

**3. Which known-in-advance variables carry signal?**
- SNAP days: +6.1% pooled, positive at nine of ten stores (WI_3 +12.2%,
  WI_2 +10.8%, CA_3 +8.4%, CA_1 +7.9%, TX_1 +6.2%, TX_3 +5.0%, TX_2 +4.8%,
  CA_4 +2.3%, WI_1 +1.3%, CA_2 −0.2%). Usable.
- Calendar events, measured against a same-weekday baseline: Thanksgiving
  1.70× on the day and 1.41× the day after; Christmas 1.70× two days before,
  1.47× the day before, closed on the day, 1.20× the day after; Labor Day
  1.36×; Independence Day 1.20× with a 1.27× run-up; Valentine's Day 1.18×
  with a 1.29× run-up; Easter 1.11× with a 1.23× run-up; New Year 0.85×.
  Those seven are the major events. The other 23 sit within ±12% of baseline
  (Chanukah End clears the bar numerically but falls inside the Christmas
  run-up and is excluded as confounded).
- Price: three values in five years (1.48 to Feb 2012, 1.58 to Aug 2014, 1.68
  after), changed on the same dates at every store, never inside a forecast
  window. A clock, not a variable. Switched on for XGBoost it scores 0.674
  against 0.665 off. Left off.

**4. Does the demand class vary across stores?**
No. Every store is `smooth`: ADI is pinned at 1.00 by volume, and CV² runs
0.06–0.24 against a cut of 0.49. The study therefore answers "which method
wins on a fast mover, and does it depend on the store," not "does the best
method change with demand class." The within-corner spread (CV² 0.06 at CA_3
to 0.24 at WI_2, volumes 14 to 103 a day) is the gradient the comparison runs
along.

## 02_evaluate — which methods beat the benchmarks, and where?

**1. Which methods beat the benchmarks, and by how much?**
Mean RMSSE over 520 series-folds (all / normal weeks / holiday weeks; bias in
units per day, positive = over-forecast):

| method | all | normal | holiday | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARIMA (seasonal, calendar regressors) | 0.651 | 0.631 | 0.735 | −0.09 |
| XGBoost | 0.665 | 0.653 | 0.717 | +0.88 |
| ETS (Holt-Winters) | 0.674 | 0.643 | 0.802 | −0.14 |
| moving average (28) | 0.797 | 0.778 | 0.873 | −0.07 |
| seasonal naïve (last week) | 0.861 | 0.822 | 1.024 | −0.07 |
| mean | 0.962 | 0.955 | 0.994 | +3.27 |
| seasonal naïve (last year) | 1.038 | 1.028 | 1.081 | −0.15 |
| naïve | 1.099 | 1.098 | 1.101 | +9.54 |
| drift | 1.100 | 1.099 | 1.102 | +9.55 |

The three models are within 0.023 of each other (medians 0.60–0.64: a tie
on ordinary weeks). All three beat the strongest benchmark by 0.12–0.15 and
the human default screen (seasonal naïve) by 0.19–0.21, roughly 24% lower
scaled error. Win rates against seasonal naïve: ARIMA 82%, ETS 83%, XGBoost
75% of series-folds; against the 28-day mean: 75%, 71%, 72%.

**2. Does the ranking depend on the store, and on the kind of week?**
Yes to both.
- Store (all weeks): XGBoost best at TX_2, TX_3, TX_1, WI_3, WI_1; ARIMA best
  at CA_3, CA_1, CA_2, WI_2; ETS best at CA_4. Every model beats every
  benchmark at every store except CA_4 (18/day), where the 28-day mean
  (0.577) is level with ARIMA (0.575) and ETS (0.562) and ahead of XGBoost
  (0.719).
- Week kind: on normal weeks the classical models edge XGBoost (ARIMA 0.631,
  ETS 0.643, XGBoost 0.653). On holiday weeks XGBoost leads (0.717) and is
  best at seven of ten stores; ETS falls furthest (0.802), the one model that
  cannot be told a holiday is coming. The ARIMA–ETS gap on holiday weeks,
  0.735 against 0.802, is the measured value of the calendar regressors.
- "This day last year" (lag-364 naïve) is worse than "this day last week"
  everywhere, holiday weeks included (1.081 vs 1.024): the level drifted and
  last year's lookup carries the old level.

**3. How does error grow with horizon?**
From h = 1 to h = 7: XGBoost 0.64 → 0.80, ARIMA 0.64 → 0.77, ETS 0.66 → 0.77;
the 28-day mean 0.75 → 1.03 and seasonal naïve 0.88 → 0.97. The models'
edge is largest at h = 1–4 and narrows at the weekend days. Caveat stated in
the notebook: every origin is a Sunday, so h = 6 is always Saturday and
h = 7 always Sunday, the two busiest days; the rise mixes horizon with
weekday.

**4. Are the residuals clean?**
At TX_2 over the year (forecast − actual): XGBoost mean +3.94, sd 15.6; ETS
+0.43, sd 18.7; ARIMA +0.42, sd 17.1. Lag-1 autocorrelation 0.29 / 0.41 /
0.35 and Ljung-Box p < 0.001 for all three: expected for 1–7-step errors
from a shared origin, which are correlated to lag 6 even for a perfect
model. Lag-7 autocorrelation is 0.08 / 0.04 / 0.13: no weekly pattern left.
XGBoost's bias is the one real finding: +3.9/day at TX_2 (about 4% of the
level), +2.7 at CA_4 and TX_3, +2.6 at CA_1, −3.6 at CA_2, while ARIMA and
ETS stay within ±0.5 at nine of ten stores. Split by day type, XGBoost
over-forecasts ordinary days (+1.1) and under-forecasts holiday-window days
(−2.1); every model under-forecasts the top-decile days by 14–15 units.

**5. Where do the models lose?**
The 28-day mean beats XGBoost in 28% of series-folds, ETS in 29%, ARIMA in
25%; seasonal naïve beats XGBoost in 25%, ETS and ARIMA in 17%. The losses
concentrate at the quietest store (CA_4) and in the weeks after a level shift
(the TX_2 drop in late August 2015 and the January 2016 trough are visible
in the forecast plot as two to three weeks where every method lags).

## 03_order — from forecast to order quantity

**1. How often would ordering the mean have stocked out?**
Share of scored weeks in which the weekly total was under-forecast: ARIMA,
ETS, the 28-day mean and seasonal naïve 50%; XGBoost 44% (its positive bias
buys 6 units a week of cover); the plain mean 39% and naïve/drift 25%, but
only by over-ordering 23 and 67 units a week. Weekly-total error sd: XGBoost
42.6, ARIMA 45.5, ETS 51.4, seasonal naïve 51.4 units.

**2. Does the ranking survive an asymmetric cost?**
Relative pinball loss (÷ mean weekly sales), calibrated on May 2014–May 2015,
scored on May 2015–May 2016; lower is better, compare within a column:

| method | τ 0.3 | τ 0.5 | τ 0.7 | τ 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARIMA | 0.094 | 0.106 | 0.096 | 0.053 |
| XGBoost | 0.114 | 0.116 | 0.104 | 0.052 |
| moving average (28) | 0.105 | 0.117 | 0.107 | 0.062 |
| ETS | 0.104 | 0.120 | 0.107 | 0.059 |
| seasonal naïve | 0.109 | 0.123 | 0.110 | 0.061 |

No. ARIMA is best at 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7 and ties XGBoost at 0.9 (0.053 vs
0.052). XGBoost is second at 0.7 and 0.9 and fifth at 0.3, behind two
benchmarks. On holiday weeks ARIMA and XGBoost are clearly ahead at every τ
(0.123 / 0.127 at 0.5 against 0.152 for ETS and the moving average). With an
expanding calibration window XGBoost recovers to second at every τ (0.099
at 0.3): its errors shrink as training data grows, so a fixed prior year
overstates its spread.

**3. Are the quantiles trustworthy?**
Coverage at τ = 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9: ARIMA 0.27 / 0.49 / 0.68 / 0.92;
XGBoost 0.30 / 0.48 / 0.69 / 0.92; ETS 0.28 / 0.51 / 0.72 / 0.89; 28-day
mean 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.71 / 0.90. Within three points of target everywhere for
the models. The plain mean is the exception (0.83 at τ = 0.9): its bias is
not stable across years.

**4. What would the order have been?**
At TX_2 with ARIMA, the τ = 0.9 order-up-to level sits a constant 116 units
a week above the median forecast (the two offsets are fixed for the year, so
their gap is too). The actual rose above it in 1 of 52 weeks, the week
beginning 6 July 2015, so the realised service level was 98% against a 90%
target at this store; across all stores and methods the models ran short in
8–11% of weeks at τ = 0.9. The January 2016 trough is the opposite failure:
a month where every method's median sat above what sold, and a 0.9 order
would have carried surplus every week.
